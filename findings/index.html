Why change fails: what 816 sources say

The Why Change Fails evidence base collects 816 verified sources from 603 publishers on why organizational transformations and AI programs fail. Each source is mapped to one or more of the Five Breakpoints, the predictable ways large-scale change breaks down, with a sentence citing the source's own finding: 2,377 citations in all. 215 of the sources come from high-credibility publishers.

Updated 2026-11-25. Maintained by Brandon Freitag; the argument it supports is published at Work That Holds.

527 of 816 sources (65%)

Vague purpose producing illusion of alignment. Leadership fails to define what the transformation must deliver.

Momentum Mirage

472 of 816 sources (58%)

Appearance of progress without actual movement. Activity and busy-ness without changed work or outcomes.

Recent high-credibility evidence

  • Artificial Intelligence in the Firm: Bottlenecks in Software Production Harvard University (Chen & Stratton, job market paper) · 2026-11-14

    Code, commits and pull requests all rise by a third to a half while deployed issues and completed epics do not significantly move, so the function that instruments itself most densely registers 44% more of what it counts and no more of what it ships.

    98% have formally assigned board-level responsibility for AI risk, but only 26% have a dedicated board-level AI committee, only 37% have a board-reviewed quantification of the financial exposure, and the modal answer on who is accountable for an agent's adverse outcome is CIO/CTO at 17% with no consensus —…

  • The Adaptive Advantage: Who's Winning With AI and How — 2026 AI Adoption Report (Year Four) Wharton Human-AI Research / GBK Collective · 2026-10-13

    Positive return is reported as near-universal at 80% while strongly positive return reaches only 28%, departments that never formally measured return are counted as not having shown one, and sentiment moves against usage with cautious +7pp, skeptical +8pp and excited -8pp year over year.

  • The Rapid Adoption of Generative AI Management Science (INFORMS) 72(10):8440-8456 — Bick, Blandin & Deming · 2026-10-01

    Work adoption of genAI outpaced the personal computer and overall adoption outpaced both PCs and the internet, and that record-setting diffusion curve coexists with a 1.4% time saving, which is adoption velocity being read as organizational movement where the activity is real and the movement is not yet.
