Why change fails: what 738 sources say

The Why Change Fails evidence base collects 738 verified sources from 536 publishers on why organizational transformations and AI programs fail. Each source is mapped to one or more of the Five Breakpoints, the predictable ways large-scale change breaks down, with a sentence citing the source's own finding: 2,220 citations in all. 183 of the sources come from high-credibility publishers.

Updated 2026-10-26. Maintained by Brandon Freitag; the argument it supports is published at Work That Holds.

Strategic Disconnection

505 of 738 sources (68%)

Vague purpose producing illusion of alignment. Leadership fails to define what the transformation must deliver.

Recent high-credibility evidence

    The filing specifies the intervention to the decimal (7% of workforce, $65-75M, complete by end of Q1 FY2027) while stating the intended outcome only as a flatter, faster organization where decisions can be made faster — the precision asymmetry Paper 1 identifies, where actions are checkable and the goal is not.

    Every differentiator between established-ROI (n=208) and experimentation-stage (n=268) organizations is a decision structure — formal harness layer 86% vs 31%, cost review at approval 73% vs 50% — while KPMG reports that attention remains fixed on adoption.

    fewer than half of companies have a dedicated AI leader responsible for AI adoption (46%) and only 52% have clear frameworks for AI development — agents are being deployed at scale with no single owner of the outcome and no shared definition of how they should be built.

Process Friction

472 of 738 sources (64%)

Structural friction blocking execution. Outdated hierarchies, unclear decision rights, bottlenecks in workflow.

    Copilot raised project-level code contributions 5.9% while coordination time for code integration rose 8%, driven by a 6.5% increase in code-discussion volume — the production step accelerated and the unchanged review-and-integration machinery absorbed the difference.

  • AI Governance and the Decentralization of Technology Production: An Investigation of AI-Based IPA Bots Information Systems Research (INFORMS) · 2026-09-01

    In a Fortune 200 multinational, traditional centralized governance mandates 'can actively suppress utilization and stifle organizational reuse' of Intelligent Process Automation bots the firm had already built and deployed — structural friction located in the governance layer, blocking execution of working automation.

  • Accenture Pulse of Change (July 2026 edition) Accenture · 2026-07-26

    36 percent of C-suite leaders and 36 percent of employees independently name middle management as the largest AI capability gap, both altitudes locating the constraint at the layer that owns handoffs and decision rights.

  • ISHIR: Production AI Is No Longer an Innovation Problem — It Is an Operational One ISHIR Blog (citing McKinsey State of AI, Gartner, Deloitte) · 2026-07-24

    the cited McKinsey figure that nearly two-thirds of organizations remain in experimentation or pilot stages, alongside Deloitte's finding that only one-third are truly redesigning business operations, shows pilots failing to scale because the operating model beneath them was never rebuilt.

Technology Illusion

501 of 738 sources (68%)

Deploying technology on top of broken organizational conditions. Mistaking capability for readiness.

Recent high-credibility evidence

  • The New Realities of AI Governance — Eight in Ten EMEA Enterprises Escalate AI Incidents to Board or C-Suite as AI Agents Test Governance IBM · 2026-10-14

    75% expect AI agents to hold some decision autonomy by 2027 while only 37% say their governance is designed to scale with agentic deployment and 56% say they continue deploying within agreed risk limits while governance adapts — the deployers themselves forecast deployment onto a substrate they describe as inadequate.

  • The Rapid Adoption of Generative AI Management Science (INFORMS) 72(10):8440-8456 — Bick, Blandin & Deming · 2026-10-01

    The same nationally representative instrument that finds 27% of employed adults using genAI for work in a given week also finds only 1%-7% of work hours assisted and self-reported time savings of 1.4% of total hours — a refereed measurement of the distance between a technology being present in the workforce and a…

  • KPMG Global AI Pulse Q3 2026 — What Scale Demands: 89% Report Measurable Value, 12% Assess That Value Against Its Cost KPMG · 2026-09-24

    89% of senior leaders report measurable value from AI while only 12% consistently assess that value against the cost it incurs, so near-universal credited benefit coexists with near-absent benefit-versus-cost adjudication.

  • The Impact of Generative AI on Collaborative Open-Source Software Development: Evidence from GitHub Copilot Information Systems Research (INFORMS) · 2026-09-24

    The tool delivered what it promised at task level (individual contributions +2.1%, coding participation +3.4%) while a material share of the gain was consumed by the surrounding process the organization did not redesign.

  • From Data-Model Fit to Model-World Fit: A Framework for Integrating Domain Knowledge into Artificial Intelligence Information Systems Research (INFORMS) · 2026-09-22

    The paper's central claim is that ML/AI systems 'often perform well in controlled settings but fail when deployed in the real world' because development optimizes data-model fit rather than model-world fit — the Technology Illusion's mechanism named from inside the engineering process, specifying what the missing…

  • Workflow Automation in Open-Source Software Development: Accelerating Innovation Through Mechanization and Orchestration Information Systems Research (INFORMS) · 2026-09-01

    Mechanization, the visible and purchasable form of automation applied to the tasks, bought 7.8% on maintenance and close to nothing on the development work organizations justify the investment with — a capability delivering its demo value and not the value it was bought for.

Momentum Mirage

444 of 738 sources (60%)

Appearance of progress without actual movement. Activity and busy-ness without changed work or outcomes.

Recent high-credibility evidence

  • The New Realities of AI Governance — Eight in Ten EMEA Enterprises Escalate AI Incidents to Board or C-Suite as AI Agents Test Governance IBM · 2026-10-14

    98% have formally assigned board-level responsibility for AI risk, but only 26% have a dedicated board-level AI committee, only 37% have a board-reviewed quantification of the financial exposure, and the modal answer on who is accountable for an agent's adverse outcome is CIO/CTO at 17% with no consensus —…

  • The Rapid Adoption of Generative AI Management Science (INFORMS) 72(10):8440-8456 — Bick, Blandin & Deming · 2026-10-01

    Work adoption of genAI outpaced the personal computer and overall adoption outpaced both PCs and the internet, and that record-setting diffusion curve coexists with a 1.4% time saving, which is adoption velocity being read as organizational movement where the activity is real and the movement is not yet.

  • KPMG Global AI Pulse Q3 2026 — What Scale Demands: 89% Report Measurable Value, 12% Assess That Value Against Its Cost KPMG · 2026-09-24

    KPMG locates the break precisely: the steepest drop in value realization comes after organizations report value, when they must realize it consistently.

  • AI Governance and the Decentralization of Technology Production: An Investigation of AI-Based IPA Bots Information Systems Research (INFORMS) · 2026-09-01

    Decentralized building past the authors' boundary conditions produced 'fragmentation in the form of one-off solutions' — bots that run but never compound into repeatability, which is the authors' own outcome variable, so activity accumulates without organizational movement.

  • Accenture Pulse of Change (July 2026 edition) Accenture · 2026-07-26

    The share of large enterprises reporting widespread, sustained business value from AI fell from 32 percent to 23 percent inside a single year of the same instrument, while 82 percent of the same leadership population increased AI investment and roughly 7 in 10 reported agentic impact exceeding expectations.

  • ISHIR: Production AI Is No Longer an Innovation Problem — It Is an Operational One ISHIR Blog (citing McKinsey State of AI, Gartner, Deloitte) · 2026-07-24

    two-thirds of organizations sitting in perpetual experimentation and pilot stages, against Gartner's observation that higher-maturity organizations keep initiatives in production significantly longer, is activity that sustains itself without converting into durable movement.
